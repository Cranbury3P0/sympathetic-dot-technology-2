# The Making of *Orientation Before Adaptation*

This directory records the development of the book as a documentary archive.

The goal is not to preserve every keystroke. It is to preserve the meaningful evolution of the work: where ideas came from, how the structure changed, what Sean discovered through writing, what was abandoned, and why decisions were made.

## What gets documented

1. **Origins** — why the book began and what Sean believed it might become.
2. **Decisions** — titles, structure, audience, voice, scope, and changes of direction.
3. **Conversations** — interviews, spoken reflections, and exchanges that materially shaped the work.
4. **Artifacts** — outlines, diagrams, marked-up drafts, index cards, screenshots, recordings, and research finds.
5. **Turning points** — discoveries that changed the argument or architecture.
6. **Failures and removals** — approaches that did not work and why they were abandoned.
7. **Authorship** — the actual practice of Sean finding the sentences and building the argument.
8. **Editorial development** — questions, structural problems, revisions, and continuity work.
9. **Publication** — design, production, permissions, proofs, and release.

## What does not need to be documented

- routine file maintenance;
- every minor wording change;
- technical operations with no effect on the book;
- private material Sean marks as off the record;
- raw repetition that does not reveal development.

## Documentary forms

The archive should support several possible final forms without deciding among them yet:

- a written making-of essay or afterword;
- a short documentary film;
- an episodic audio or video diary;
- a companion website;
- a public conversation or launch presentation;
- an annotated edition showing the development of key ideas.

## Capture rule

Record the process at the moment of decision whenever possible. Retrospective memory may supplement the archive, but it should not replace contemporaneous notes.
