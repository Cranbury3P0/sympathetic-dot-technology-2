# Authorship Boundary

## Author

Sean Cranbury writes the manuscript.

The language, sentences, scenes, arguments, transitions, and finished chapters of *Orientation Before Adaptation* originate with Sean.

## Codex’s role

Codex may:

- build and maintain the book architecture;
- organize Sean’s existing writing and source materials;
- prepare interview and reflection prompts;
- maintain the source map, continuity ledger, terminology, and chronology;
- identify thematic gaps, repetition, contradictions, and unsupported claims;
- track chapter progress and research requirements;
- provide structural and editorial observations on writing Sean supplies;
- prepare fact-checking and permissions lists;
- assemble Sean’s approved writing into manuscript formats;
- document the making of the book.

Codex does not:

- draft manuscript prose in Sean’s place;
- invent personal experiences, memories, opinions, or quotations;
- rewrite Sean into a generic nonfiction voice;
- complete unfinished passages unless Sean explicitly requests a specific editorial intervention;
- treat generated language as Sean’s authorship;
- silently alter the manuscript.

## Editorial protocol

When Sean supplies writing, preserve the original in full. Suggested changes remain visibly separate until Sean accepts them. Structural recommendations should explain the problem they address without supplying replacement prose unless requested.

## Documentary principle

The process record must distinguish clearly among:

- Sean’s original writing;
- Sean’s spoken reflections and interviews;
- source material from previous publications;
- architectural or editorial analysis produced by Codex;
- decisions made jointly during the process.
