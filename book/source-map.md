# Source Map

## Audit summary

The present Sympathetic Technology corpus contains approximately 24,000 words of mixed prose and interface code across the home page, three system pages, seven Field Notes essays, and the Harvard capstone case study. The extractable finished prose is substantial enough to establish the book’s argument, vocabulary, and public voice, but not enough to produce the final book by simple expansion.

The material is strongest on institutional life, technology, governance, cultural authority, organizational memory, continuity, and the politics of adoption. It is weakest on Sean’s personal history, the origins of the word “sympathetic,” the lived development of his practice, and scenes from work that would make the philosophy embodied rather than merely asserted.

## Primary sources and their book functions

### Home page: “Our Approach”

Core material:

- Orientation Before Adaptation
- Connect to the Historical Present
- Use the Language of Intention
- trust, transparency, equity, self-sufficiency

Book function: establishes the opening movement and the basic method. These passages should be expanded through scenes and historical reflection, not reproduced as three service principles.

### “The AI Frontier Is Inside Your Office”

Core material:

- “The adoption is already over. It just didn’t go through governance.”
- staff use as a rational response to economic pressure;
- policy that does not shape Tuesday afternoon is not governance;
- passive decisions are still decisions.

Book function: grounds the argument in ordinary organizational life. It supplies a strong opening scene for the power section and prevents leadership from treating staff as the problem.

### “Introducing Controlled Intelligence” and the Controlled Intelligence page

Core material:

- governance before automation;
- local control before convenience;
- translation before transformation;
- explainability as a governance requirement;
- architecture makes commitments true, repeatable, and provable;
- the same technology becomes morally different under a different arrangement of custody and accountability.

Book function: demonstrates that philosophy becomes infrastructure. Controlled Intelligence should appear as an example of a governing arrangement, not a product chapter.

### “Time for Vancouver to Come Clean About AI”

Core material:

- public legitimacy and the failure to consult;
- unceded Coast Salish territory;
- ecological cost and civic ambition;
- skepticism as an earned response rather than ignorance;
- refusal of the claim that dystopia is inevitable.

Book function: supplies place, weather, conflict, public feeling, and the distinction between being asked and being told. Factual claims will require renewed verification before publication.

### “A New Cultural Embassy”

Core material:

- infrastructure can acknowledge the culture and labour from which it draws power;
- authority is structural, not decorative;
- an embassy operates under the authority of the culture it represents;
- the city already possesses knowledge it is choosing to forget;
- institutional debt can be converted into durable public benefit only through independent governance.

Book function: expands the idea of technology from tools to civic and cultural infrastructure. It is the book’s strongest constructive public vision.

### “The Pope Has Entered the Chat”

Core material:

- language stripped of social function;
- ethics in the abstract versus enforceable arrangements;
- institutional history used deliberately;
- refusal of both technological panic and technological fatalism;
- governance as moral duty;
- “What kind of humans do we intend to remain while building systems that simulate us?”

Book function: supplies the governing question and a model for reading power through institutional history. All event-specific claims must be fact-checked at manuscript revision.

### “Canada’s AI Strategy 01: Frame After Frame”

Core material:

- adoption as the hidden engine of public policy;
- trust treated as a lever rather than something earned;
- resistance reframed as a translation problem;
- the diagram as an honest disclosure of the strategy’s logic;
- “The center is a frame too.”

Book function: gives the manuscript a method of visual and rhetorical interpretation. It strengthens the argument that institutional language and diagrams reveal intentions they may not openly name.

### “The Master’s Tools Will Never Dismantle the Master’s Datacenter”

Core material:

- tools that reproduce the structures they appear to challenge;
- the communications environment becoming capable only of producing more of itself;
- cultural references as a way of thinking, not ornament;
- abandoning a tool as a legitimate beginning;
- the human need for books, friendship, art, and presence.

Book function: supplies intellectual autobiography, humour, anger, and limits. It should be used carefully beside Lorde, without collapsing her argument into a technology metaphor.

### Meridian Framework

Core material:

- the distance between what is written, what is known, and what is practised;
- knowledge, expertise and judgment, and voice;
- provenance;
- surfacing disagreement before correction becomes crisis;
- organizational identity as an ongoing conversation.

Book function: gives formal shape to the historical present and the language of intention. Meridian demonstrates why documents alone do not constitute institutional knowledge.

### Resonance Engine

Core material:

- continuity, not automation;
- clarity, not creativity;
- architecture, not application;
- presence, not productivity;
- a desk that remembers the conversation without doing the work;
- preserving reasoning between sessions.

Book function: extends the philosophy into creative life. Resonance provides the bridge from institutional memory to artistic continuity and helps articulate what supportive technology feels like.

### Harvard capstone case study

Core material:

- a tool proposal became a study of organizational readiness;
- technology was the easiest part of the problem;
- authority, trust, culturally grounded governance, and the capacity to change course cannot be automated;
- responsible adoption changes how an organization listens, decides, learns, shares power, and acts;
- start small enough to learn;
- adoption was never enough;
- responsible refusal;
- acknowledgment of absent Indigenous participation and the limits of the original proposal.

Book function: supplies the intellectual hinge between earlier practice and Sympathetic Technology. It is also evidence of Sean’s willingness to revise his own work and state its limitations directly.

## Recurring philosophical claims

1. Technology is never only a tool; it changes relationships, authority, memory, and language.
2. Adoption is not a neutral measure of success.
3. People often appear resistant when institutions have failed to earn trust or describe a meaningful benefit.
4. Governance is an arrangement of power, not a policy document.
5. History is active infrastructure.
6. Language is an intentional human act.
7. Knowledge without judgment is incomplete; judgment without provenance is fragile; voice without alignment is performance.
8. Continuity supports human work better than automation that tries to replace it.
9. A system should increase the user’s capacity and self-sufficiency.
10. Refusal is a legitimate outcome of orientation.
11. Consultation cannot substitute for authority.
12. The future is not inevitable merely because someone has financed it.
