# Documentary Production Architecture

## Parallel works

The project produces two related but distinct bodies of work:

### The book

Written by Sean Cranbury. The finished philosophical argument.

### The process archive

A contemporaneous record of how the book was conceived, structured, written, revised, and published.

The process archive serves the documentary. It must never turn Sean’s writing practice into a performance that distorts the book itself.

## Documentary narrative

The making-of story has a natural dramatic structure:

### Act I — Recovering the philosophy

- The idea of writing a book emerges from the Sympathetic Technology website.
- Existing systems and essays reveal a coherent worldview.
- The question changes from “Is there a book?” to “What is the book actually about?”
- Sean establishes the authorship boundary: architecture can be assisted; writing remains his.

### Act II — Finding the form

- Existing public writing is mapped into themes.
- Personal history exposes what the public work cannot yet explain.
- The outline changes as Sean writes rather than merely filling predetermined containers.
- Tensions emerge: philosophy versus company, critique versus construction, technology versus relationship, public argument versus personal truth.

### Act III — Making the argument hold

- Chapters begin to speak to one another.
- Repetition becomes either resonance or a problem.
- Claims are researched and fact-checked.
- Sean confronts what must be revised, removed, qualified, or made more personal.

### Act IV — The object enters the world

- The manuscript becomes a designed book.
- Permissions, notes, images, and production choices become part of the philosophy.
- The publication process tests the stated commitments to custody, culture, labour, and intention.
- The book is released without pretending the inquiry is finished.

## Capture streams

### 1. Session log

A dated record of meaningful work sessions:

- what Sean intended to work on;
- what actually happened;
- discoveries;
- difficulties;
- decisions;
- next questions.

### 2. Decision log

One entry per consequential choice. Each records:

- the decision;
- alternatives considered;
- evidence or reasoning;
- who made it;
- whether it is reversible;
- the chapters or artifacts affected.

### 3. Sean’s voice notes

Short audio or video reflections recorded before or after selected writing sessions. These should capture thought while it is still unsettled, not polished summaries designed to sound definitive.

### 4. Interviews

Longer recorded conversations organized around personal history, work, place, intellectual influences, and the emergence of each major idea.

### 5. Visual record

- workspace and writing environment;
- handwritten notes and diagrams;
- book stacks and source texts;
- Vancouver locations relevant to the argument;
- Sympathetic Technology diagrams and their development;
- marked-up pages at major revision stages.

### 6. Version milestones

Preserve selected snapshots rather than every save:

- first architecture;
- first writing in each chapter;
- complete rough chapter;
- structurally revised chapter;
- line-edited chapter;
- copy-edited chapter;
- page proof.

### 7. Research and verification trail

Maintain the sources behind externally verifiable claims, quotations, permissions, and corrections. Record when evidence changes the manuscript.

## Ethical boundaries

- Sean decides what is on the record, off the record, or embargoed.
- Client identities and confidential organizational material remain protected.
- No private conversation enters the documentary merely because it was captured.
- Indigenous people and governance are not used as narrative validation for Sean’s philosophy.
- Contributors approve how their recorded participation is represented.
- The documentary records uncertainty without manufacturing conflict.
- The making-of process does not convert every intimate experience into content.

## Minimum capture rhythm

### Every meaningful writing session

- a two-minute opening note: intention and current question;
- a two-minute closing note: what changed and what remains unresolved;
- automatic preservation of the day’s manuscript version;
- one brief session-log entry.

### Weekly

- review chapter status;
- record one ten-to-fifteen-minute reflection;
- identify the week’s most important artifact or turning point;
- update the decision and continuity ledgers.

### At each chapter milestone

- record a focused conversation about what Sean thought the chapter would be and what it became;
- preserve the milestone draft;
- capture a marked-up copy or screen record of the structural review;
- log sources, unresolved facts, and permissions.

### At each part milestone

- conduct a longer interview about how the book’s argument has changed;
- audit repetition and continuity across chapters;
- select documentary material while the context is still available.

## Production restraint

The archive should be comprehensive enough to reconstruct the development of the book, but light enough that documenting the work does not replace doing it. A session that produces no documentary material but produces honest writing is not a failed session.
