# Decision Log

## D-001 — Book length and form

- **Decision:** Develop a 150–180-page work of first-person philosophical nonfiction.
- **Status:** Working decision; reversible.
- **Reason:** The current body of ideas supports approximately 48,000–52,000 words without requiring a comprehensive memoir or padded business-book structure.

## D-002 — Working title

- **Decision:** Use *Orientation Before Adaptation* as the working title.
- **Status:** Provisional.
- **Reason:** It expresses the book’s central intellectual move and already belongs to the Sympathetic Technology philosophy.

- **Decision:** Sean Cranbury writes the manuscript. Codex builds and maintains the architecture and may provide editorial analysis on request.
- **Status:** Fixed boundary.
- **Reason:** The book is Sean’s philosophy and must emerge through Sean’s language and act of writing.

## D-007 — “Losers Built This: Towards a Cooler Internet”

- **Decision:** Use “Losers Built This: Towards a Cooler Internet” as the provisional title of Chapter 11, opening Part IV.
- **Status:** Provisional title; architectural placement active.
- **Replaces:** “The Master’s Datacenter.”
- **Reason:** The title is original to Sean’s project, carries his sharper cultural voice, and allows the chapter to turn from critique of the existing communications environment toward the construction of a more desirable internet.
- **Important interpretation:** “Losers” refers upward to the powerful people and institutions that made the internet extractive, compulsory, repetitive, and uncool. It must not read as contempt for ordinary users living inside the system.
- **Structural consequence:** The chapter now requires Sean’s personal internet history and a constructive second movement defining “cooler” as a set of cultural, political, and architectural conditions rather than an aesthetic preference.

## D-008 — Bring McLuhan into “Towards a Cooler Internet”

- **Decision:** Use Marshall McLuhan’s hot/cool media distinction and his analysis of electronic interdependence as a major intellectual hinge in Chapter 11.
- **Status:** Active research decision.
- **Reason:** McLuhan gives “cooler” a rigorous second meaning based on participation and allows the chapter to distinguish user activity from meaningful agency.
- **Qualification:** The manuscript should say McLuhan anticipated important effects of networked electronic media, not that he produced a precise technical prediction of the World Wide Web.
- **Productive disagreement:** Sean can use McLuhan’s method while challenging his technological fatalism. Orientation must retain refusal; understanding an apparently inevitable medium is not enough.

## D-009 — Treat Field Notes as a canonical primary-source corpus

- **Decision:** Use the seven published Field Notes at <https://sympathetic.technology/field-notes> as traceable primary sources for the book architecture.
- **Status:** Active.
- **Reason:** The series contains the clearest existing public expression of Sean’s arguments about governance, adoption, authority, infrastructure, language, place, refusal, and technological lock-in.
- **Method:** Preserve the published essays as source artifacts. Map their arguments and language into the book, but do not silently rewrite them into manuscript prose.
- **Authorship consequence:** Sean decides what is reused, contradicted, expanded, or abandoned. Codex maintains provenance and identifies connections.
